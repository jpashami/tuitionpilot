01

Vision

Tuition that pays itself — on time, to the right school, never twice.

Families who support a student abroad — especially where banks restrict or delay foreign exchange — should not have to chase deadlines, trust forwarded invoices, or wait on a wire that may never leave. They set the rules once; an agent does the rest and shows proof.

Hackathon goal: show an agent that completes a real payment end to end — understand the invoice, check it, pay it, and prove it — with the safety a payments judge expects.

What we are
  • Access for families whose bank transfer is blocked, rationed or slow.
  • Guardrails: pinned payee, caps, deadline-aware autopay, one payment per invoice.
  • Proof both the family and the school can read.
What we are not
  • Not cheaper than specialist FX providers — competitive at best (see 08).
  • Not a promise of the best exchange rate — a locked, visible one.
  • Not asking universities to accept crypto — they receive CAD.
02

Problem

Money can’t always leave

In some countries banks ration or refuse foreign exchange for study abroad — Nigeria’s central bank stopped supplying it. Where wires do work they take 1–5 days and hide a 2–4% margin.

Deadlines are unforgiving

A missed due date means late fees and a registration hold — and for international students, stress about study-permit status.

Fraud targets families

Canadian police and universities report tuition scams targeting international students — one ring took more than CAD 125,000, one victim lost CAD 37,000. A parent far away can’t easily tell a real invoice from a fake one.

No shared view

The student sees the invoice, the parent holds the money, and neither gets instant proof that the school was paid.

Families hold stablecoins, not spare Bitcoin

India ranks first and Nigeria sixth for crypto adoption, and the activity is mostly USDT used for payments. Bitcoin is held as an investment. Almost no university accepts either directly.

05

Core flows

Set up a family

Parent creates the family, adds the student, picks the school from a verified list (which pins the school’s GoBTC merchant ID), and sets the cap per term, the payment window and autopay. The family wallet is created and its address shown.

Fund the wallet

Parent sends BTC from any wallet. The app shows the spendable balance and how much the next invoice needs, including the network fee.

Pay an invoice (happy path)

01 Student uploads the invoice (PDF, photo or text).
02 Agent extracts payee, merchant ID, student number, term, amount, currency, due date and reference.
03 Rule check runs in code: approved payee, student match, currency, cap per term (counting what is already committed), payment window, autopay.
04 The bursar’s payment request is created with an idempotency key derived from the invoice.
05 Agent reads the request from the payer side and re-verifies payee and amount; checks balance; builds, verifies and signs the transaction; submits.
06 GoBTC co-signs → paid. Parent and student get proof. Settlement follows.

Refuse

Unapproved payee, wrong student or currency, over the cap, too early, past due, or autopay off → nothing is signed. The agent explains which rule failed and flags any instructions hidden inside the invoice.

Exceptions

Wallet short of funds

Status “needs wallet funds”, shows the sats required, retry later.

Payment request expired

A new attempt with a new key; the old request can never be paid.

Network error after submit

Status “unknown”; reconcile with GoBTC, never resubmit.

Same invoice uploaded twice

Reported as a duplicate; nothing paid.

Model declines or fails

Invoice goes to human review.

07

Paid vs. settled

Paid Seconds

GoBTC accepted the agent’s signature and committed to co-sign. The coins can’t move without GoBTC, so they can’t be spent elsewhere.

Product doesMarks tuition committed; tells the family the deadline is met

Settled ~12 hours

Transaction confirmed on Bitcoin; paidAt and transaction IDs present. About 12 hours on average, sometimes days.

Product doesIssues the final receipt

Policy

Acting on “paid” is right for tuition because the co-signing guarantee removes double-spend risk, and the family’s real concern is the deadline. The final receipt waits for settlement.

Exchange rate and fees

The university asks for a fixed CAD amount. GoBTC converts it to sats at the current price and holds that price for 30 minutes. The family pays exactly those sats.

Conclusion

We do not claim to be cheaper, and we cannot guarantee the best exchange rate on a volatile asset. We claim speed, access where wires fail, and a rate that is locked at request and shown to everyone. A stablecoin rail is what makes a firm CAD amount possible.

Who carries the risk

Family

Price moves while BTC waits in the family wallet before the invoice is paid. Bitcoin’s volatility was about 48% annualised in 2025, with a 36% drop in October — so the wallet should be topped up just before a due date, not months ahead.

University

Price moves between “paid” and conversion, plus the off-ramp fee. Converting immediately keeps this to minutes.

Proposed

Add a buffer (for example 1–2%) to each payment so the school always receives the full CAD amount, and refund any surplus to the family wallet. Alternatively, encourage just-in-time top-ups so the family holds BTC for days, not months.

In the demo, amounts are scaled 1 : 5,000 (the CAD 8,450 invoice is paid as CAD 1.69 ≈ 1,603 sats), and the conversion uses the live mempool.space price with a 1% fee.

09

Business model

Hypotheses to validate — not decided.

Family fee per payment

A small percentage with a minimum. Priced for access and protection — families whose wire failed compare us with a late fee, not with Convera.

Subscription

Monthly fee for families paying tuition plus a living allowance.

Partner side

Universities and education agents who want faster, verifiable international payments and fewer holds.

Beachhead

One corridor first: Nigeria → Canada, where FX rationing and stablecoin use are both well documented.

Compliance and risk

Risk
Mitigation
Canada treats dealing in virtual currency as a money services business
Register with FINTRAC before operating (written compliance programme required since March 2026; travel-rule data from CAD 1,000; large-transaction reports from CAD 10,000) — or operate through a licensed partner. KYC on parents.
Client identification (FINTRAC)
Built: the payer is verified before any CAD 1,000+ transfer — passport/driver’s licence/provincial ID/PR card, or two independent sources. A study permit is not photo ID (IRCC document without a photo) and is used only for eligibility. A self-paying student is verified as the payer. Records kept 5 years after the relationship ends.
Privacy (PIPEDA, not FOIPOP)
Novalycs is a private company, so federal PIPEDA applies (Nova Scotia’s FOIPOP covers public bodies only). Built: consent by purpose, access and export on demand, withdrawal of optional consents, erasure with a documented legal hold; access requests answered within 30 days; privacy officer named.
Sanctions and AML
Screen senders and countries; block sanctioned jurisdictions; travel-rule data where required
Invoice fraud and prompt injection
Payee pinned by the parent; rules in code; invoice text treated as data; suspicious content flagged
AI misreads an invoice
Rule check catches mismatches; human review for new schools and large amounts
Agent key compromise
Key in a managed secret store or HSM; 2-of-3 wallet with recovery key; only rule-approved payment requests are signed
Price volatility
Rate locked at request; immediate conversion; buffer option; stablecoin rail next
Discount-tuition scams paid outside the product
Out of scope: we only pay invoices from the pinned bursar. Explain the pattern to families during onboarding.
Crypto-funded tuition may face more questions in study-permit checks
Exportable receipts with payment IDs, rates and timestamps; validate with immigration advisers before launch.
Universities won’t hold crypto
Never ask them to: pay CAD through an off-ramp partner (Flywire already accepts USDC/USDT and settles in local currency).
